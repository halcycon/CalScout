CalScout privacy policy
Last updated: 7 November 2026

This policy applies to CalScout, the CalDAV discovery extension maintained by
Adam Camp (CuckooAdam).

What CalScout accesses

CalScout reads native calendar subscription settings and the CalDAV server
metadata needed to discover calendars you can access. This includes calendar
names, collection URLs, owner identifiers, resource identifiers and advertised
permissions. When you choose to find configured mail accounts, it reads mail
identity addresses to suggest calendar servers. It does not read mail messages.

CalScout does not fetch or edit calendar events itself. When you subscribe,
Thunderbird's native CalDAV provider handles event data, editing, reminders,
authentication and offline synchronization under Thunderbird's own settings.

What stays on your device

CalScout stores account usernames and server URLs, discovered calendar metadata,
subscription IDs, ignore/unsubscribe decisions, preferences, scan timestamps,
error categories and a bounded local diagnostic log in Thunderbird's extension
storage. These records support rediscovery and prevent unwanted subscriptions.

Passwords and authentication tokens are not saved in CalScout's extension storage
or diagnostic log. Native Thunderbird authentication components handle credential
lookup, password prompts and OAuth. The privileged discovery bridge checks for a
matching DAV login and uses authentication in memory for requests to that server.
It does not copy an IMAP password or send credentials to the developer.

Network connections

CalScout sends calendar-discovery requests to calendar server origins configured
or selected by you, including origins imported from existing native CalDAV
subscriptions. Server lookup can query your system DNS resolver for CalDAV service
records for your email domain. Native OAuth authentication may contact your
provider's authorization service. Those providers receive the information needed
to authenticate you and answer requests, including your network address.

Remote calendar connections require HTTPS. An explicit development option permits
HTTP only on loopback addresses for a local test server. Authenticated discovery
redirects and advertised resources are restricted to the configured origin.

CalScout sends no analytics, advertising identifiers, usage reports or diagnostic
logs to its developer or a telemetry service. It adds no tracking cookies. Websites
you open from links, calendar providers and Thunderbird's own update/telemetry
features have their own privacy practices.

Your choices and retention

You can pause accounts or automatic discovery, choose approval instead of automatic
subscription, ignore or restore calendars, and clear the diagnostic log. Other
configuration and discovery records remain locally until CalScout's extension
storage is removed, for example through normal uninstallation.

Disabling or uninstalling CalScout intentionally preserves native Thunderbird
calendar subscriptions and their cached data. Remove subscriptions through
Thunderbird's normal calendar controls if you no longer want them. Calendar
providers determine retention of data held on their servers.
